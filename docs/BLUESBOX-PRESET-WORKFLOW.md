# BluesBox preset audition and revision

This is the canonical workflow for refining the fitted V48 preset bank and developing new programs from guitar stems. Artist choices and timing estimates are starting points. The player's listening notes decide the final voicing.

1. Back up the entire live bank, including which slots contain saved overrides. Start from the live values, since a saved preset can differ from its factory version. Keep artist presets and user templates identifiable.
2. Choose two or more representative timestamps per sound. Separate cleaner, rhythm and lead sections when they have audibly different attack, drive or ambience. Save the section labels and recording version. Keep stems and captures local unless redistribution has been authorized.
3. Feed the same dry performance through the BlackBox and capture the analogue output at fixed interface gain. Include a second phrase that exercises picking transients and register changes. Reuse the same DI for before and after. Keep the raw level readings and normalize audition files separately.
4. Use FFT band measurements to test concrete requests such as less midrange or more upper edge. A comparison between different performances describes their spectral balance. For frequency response, feed the same low-level broadband signal through both configurations and retain only bins with adequate input/output coherence. For drive changes, use a fixed-level sine alongside guitar takes and compare harmonic energy.
5. Set quarter-note delay time with 60,000 divided by the tempo in BPM. Verify the beat by listening to the same recording. Check half-time and double-time interpretations in slow blues. A tempo estimate can set a musical delay time even when the stem cannot identify the delay used on the record.
6. Measure delay and reverb through the pedal with three mono Volt captures of the same short band-limited burst. Capture with both time effects off, delay only, and reverb only. Leave the amp and drive settings fixed, allow the previous tail to decay, and bypass modulation for this measurement. Use a long enough silence after the burst for the decay to reach the measurement floor.

   ```sh
   python tools/blues_timefx.py dry.wav delay.wav reverb.wav --expected-ms 780
   ```

   The tool aligns the direct bursts, removes their shared contribution, and looks for a delayed copy in the delay capture. It reports first-repeat time and level relative to the direct burst. The isolated reverb capture gives tail level and an RT60 estimate extrapolated from a measured 20 dB decay when the slope is usable. A missing RT60 can coexist with a clearly detected reverb tail. Include a dry control to check false detections. On a guitar stem, repeated notes, layered guitars and bleed can all resemble echoes. Check several note endings for the same repeat interval before calling a repeat identified. An exposed decay is evidence of ambience, with the exact unit and settings unresolved unless corroborated.
7. Audition the revision at matched loudness and check the original output level with the same DI. Use the hardware counters to check missed DSP deadlines, wet-lane cuts and clipping. Revise unsafe levels before saving. Verify the preset contents after a flash commit and export the bank, its predecessor and the actual settings tested.
8. Put each revised song preset on a local comparison page with the source timestamps, old and new guitar captures, FFT plots, and isolated time-effect measurements when available. Ask the player for the slot, pickup and first thing to change.

The SRV bank uses the existing Dimension block for chorus and plate reverb as its default ambience. Keep the rotary and Uni-Vibe sounds where they are central to the patch. Richard Mullen recalls using Dimension D while mixing *Texas Flood*. Vaughan described using a Leslie for chorus-like sounds live. The V48 guitar path sums the Dimension taps to mono. [Mullen interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/stevie-ray-vaughan-producer-richard-mullen) · [Vaughan interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/artists/stevie-ray-vaughan-discusses-fame-hendrix-and-his-new-album-soul-soul-1985-guitar-world-interview)

Lead programs use a quarter-note delay as an audition default. Set the repeats and mix for each part, and prefer a repeat interval supported by several stem excerpts when it differs from the tempo default. For example, the revised Reconsider Baby lead uses roughly 326 ms based on a recurring feature near 320 ms in three lead excerpts. Preserve the simple clean templates and back up existing user slots before revising a whole bank.
